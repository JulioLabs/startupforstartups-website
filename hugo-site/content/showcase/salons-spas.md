<div class="sc-chapter-head">
<span class="sc-step">01</span>
<div><div class="rp-eyebrow">Fill chairs, not just inboxes</div>
<h2 class="rp-h2">Weddings and events, booked from one form.</h2></div>
</div>
<div class="sc-split">
<div class="sc-copy">
<h3>A request form that asks what the stylists need to know</h3>
<p>Bridal parties used to arrive as long text threads and missed calls. Hazel &amp; Hue's request form asks for the occasion, how many people need hair done, the date and the details, such as the venue, a trial or barbering for the groom's side. Nora replies within a day with a plan and a price, and every request lands in the dashboard and the salon inbox at once.</p>
<ul class="sc-list">
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> Text, email, dropdown and free-text fields</li>
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> Styled to the site — no "embedded form" look</li>
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> A short contact form in the footer for everything else</li>
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> Requests can go straight to your booking software with webhooks</li>
</ul>
</div>

</div>
<div class="sc-split sc-split-reverse">
<div class="sc-copy">
<h3>Turn first visits into regulars</h3>
<p>New clients get 20% off their first color service, with a free consultation first, in a banner across the top of every page. When the season changes, Hazel &amp; Hue swaps the offer from the dashboard, with no website update. A one-question poll asks visitors whether they'd like a text when it's time to rebook, and a feedback tab catches anyone who couldn't find the spa menu or a stylist.</p>
