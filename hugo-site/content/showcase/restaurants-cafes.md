<section class="sc-chapter">

<div class="sc-chapter-head">
<span class="sc-step">01</span>
<div><div class="rp-eyebrow">Fill tables, not just inboxes</div>
<h2 class="rp-h2">Catering, big tables and supper club, in one form.</h2></div>
</div>
<div class="sc-split">
<div class="sc-copy">
<h3>A request form that asks what the kitchen needs to know</h3>
<p>Office breakfasts, birthday brunches for twelve and supper club seats used to arrive as voicemails and Instagram messages. Marigold's request form asks what's being planned, how many people, the date and any dietary needs, so the owner can reply the same day with a real answer. Every request lands in the dashboard and the café inbox at once.</p>
<ul class="sc-list">
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> Text, email, dropdown and free-text fields</li>
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> Styled to the site — no "embedded form" look</li>
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> A short contact form in the footer for everything else</li>
<li><i class="fas fa-check"></i> Requests can go straight to your catering or booking software with webhooks</li>
</ul>
</div>

</div>
<div class="sc-split sc-split-reverse">
<div class="sc-copy">
<h3>Fill the quiet hours with specials</h3>
<p>The early bird deal, any breakfast plate and a drip coffee for $12 on weekday mornings, runs as a banner across the top of every page. When the late-summer menu launches, Marigold swaps the banner from the dashboard, with no website update. A one-question poll asks whether guests would come for Sunday dinner, and a feedback tab catches anyone who couldn't find the allergens or the hours.</p>
